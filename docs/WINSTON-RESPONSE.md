# Winston feedback — response and change of scope

The user's subsequent instruction is to replace the complex simulator with a simple advocacy walkthrough. The main site now follows that instruction. It does not claim that the old engine has become a macroeconomic model.

## Fiscal scale

The concern is valid. The old £1,025bn envelope set was synthetic and was not reconciled to total managed expenditure. OBR March 2026 Table A.7 gives £1,368bn TME for 2025–26 and £1,419bn for 2026–27 (forecast, cash). Gross differences from £1,025bn are £343bn and £394bn respectively, before any adjustment for interest or scope. It would be wrong simply to add that difference to Everything else: a proper rebuild needs a full accounting bridge and a defined fiscal year. The new main journey quotes no synthetic spending total.

Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69a6d7b62e1f4fbda4252208/economic-and-fiscal-outlook-march-2026-web-accessible.pdf (Table A.7).

## Behaviour, safeguards and commitments

Agreed: the old engine reconciles assumed policy costs; it does not estimate a general-equilibrium, labour-supply, Laffer, capital-productivity or gilt-market response. Capacity/household points are uncalibrated design constraints. Defence commitments, ageing and state-pension policy are not complete in that engine. No electoral prediction is made. Rather than add arbitrary multipliers to satisfy the objections, the new journey removes numerical scores and presents the intended economic mechanisms, alongside their conditions. This is a different product, not a repaired fiscal forecast.

## Migration and the wage report

The old £51,500 figure is the arithmetic average of a £36,000 care-worker lifetime cost and a £67,000 adult Health and Care dependant cost. It is not an estimate for all lower-paid migrants or all non-workers. OBR's cited low-wage analysis is September 2024, with a March 2025 supplementary methodology release; it models a representative migrant with particular age/earnings assumptions, not the same care/dependant cohort. The estimates cannot be substituted solely because one is larger. No claim about a precise OBR lifetime value is made without matching its horizon and assumptions.

The reported wage-toggle bug was not reproduced in our v4 checks: selecting Historical gives +0.040% in the lower scenario and survives resume, sharing and JSON download. One initial test saw an empty string because the scenario disclosure was collapsed; opening it resolved that test. This does not establish what happened in Winston's session. The new walkthrough removes that wage toggle and does not promise a universal wage gain.

The new migration screen instead provides transparent people-flow stress tests at +171,000 (reference), zero, −100,000 and −250,000 net annually. Only the reference total flows are sourced to ONS. Reductions in lower-paid and non-working arrivals, additional qualifying removals and the unauthorised-population baseline are explicit scenario assumptions. They are not costed with the old care-route multiplier. Lawful removal capacity, eligible stocks and economic responses remain unestimated; negative net migration is not represented as proof of higher GDP per person.
