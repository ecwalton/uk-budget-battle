# Response to the second cross-check

The game remains an uncalibrated training prototype. Scenario v3 changes scoring and investment accounting; previous version links are rejected rather than silently rescored.

- **V1 — implemented:** cumulative primary savings in years 1–5 must reach £100bn, alongside £40bn in year 5. Early welfare/income/VAT action saves £222bn before interest versus £54bn for the late equivalent. The review's £235bn early figure includes interest and is not the new scored measure.
- **V2 — implemented as a disclosed design choice:** household floors are −4 for the lowest two income groups, −5 for the others. Five welfare squeezes fail. Welfare remains a transfer with zero capacity points; no empirical calibration is claimed. Phasing means five squeezes save £72bn in year 5 and £80bn subsequently, not £80bn immediately.
- **V3 — implemented:** investment is an outstanding stack of signed steps. An opposite decision cancels the most recent step first. Only remaining positive steps incur £3bn annual maintenance from their fourth year; only remaining negative steps incur a £6bn catch-up bill in their fourth year. Cancellation happens before that year's billing. Past costs and their debt interest remain. Capacity and household effects retain their disclosed two-year lag. The UI ledger and bulletin use engine group costs. Tests cover both reversals, partial restoration, bills already paid and all 243 five-step paths.
- **V4 — clarified and tested:** stable persistent packages naturally pass a legacy target equal to five times the annual target. It is a screen for future costs. A new concrete counterexample cuts welfare in Budget 1, defence in 2, other services in 3, holds in 4, and cuts investment and income tax in 5. It passes funding, term (£156bn), year 5 (£40bn) and safeguards, but fails legacy (£194bn) because of the investment catch-up bill. It contains no late spending increase.
- **V5 — implemented:** each spending control labels hold as cash hold and explains no inflation protection, while earlier ramps/bills continue. Four percent nominal GDP growth is not an inflation assumption; this model cannot support the review's precise 18% real-cut estimate.
- **V6 — outstanding user evidence:** removed the unverified 5–8 minute claim. Retained five envelopes and nine settings per Budget because the user's requested flow calls for a complete settlement and funding decision. Rotating away envelopes would change that design. The study below should inform a subsequent change; automated browser checks cannot establish human completion time.

## Ten-person timing study protocol — not yet performed

Recruit ten consenting adults with mixed familiarity with public finance. Use five phone and five desktop sessions. Ask each to complete one term without coaching, explaining that it is a training game with synthetic figures. Record device, prior familiarity, start-to-results time, completion or abandonment, pauses, help opened, and facilitator assistance. Do not collect identifying information in the study sheet.

Afterwards ask participants what Hold does, whether the borrowing allowance supplies revenue, why a plan may pass year 5 but fail the term or legacy, and whether the numbers are official estimates. Record their answers verbatim and code each as correct, partial or incorrect. Ask which decision was hardest and whether any screen felt repetitive.

Report all ten completion times, median and range, completion rate, device differences and comprehension counts. A proposed usability goal is at least eight unaided completions within eight minutes and at least eight correct answers on cash hold and borrowing. Treat this small sample as formative evidence, not statistical validation. If repetitive work dominates, test an explicit 'keep prior settlement' shortcut before removing access to envelopes. Do not reinstate a timing claim until observed results support it.
