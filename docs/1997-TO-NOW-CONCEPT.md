# Alternative concept: how today's baseline was built

Working title: **The Budget Before the Budget**

Opening: “Today's spending baseline was built over decades. What commitments, shocks and choices put it there?” This asks the user's intended question without the unverified claim that every other simulator begins in the wrong year.

A separate historical walkthrough would start in 1997 and follow changes in the scale and composition of the state through public-service expansion, the financial crisis, fiscal consolidation, pension commitments, the pandemic, the energy shock and higher debt-servicing costs. Those periods are a research structure, not a claim that a single policy caused the entire change.

Each stop should show three comparable measures: spending as a share of GDP, inflation-adjusted spending per person, and a consistently defined debt-interest series. Cash pounds can be supplementary. Put policy commitments beside demographic, price and interest-rate drivers. A date-aligned fiscal-year series should distinguish actual outturn from forecast and use one current data vintage.

Use Treasury PESA/public spending statistics, OBR Public Finances Databank and fiscal risks publications, ONS population and GDP series. The March 2026 OBR forecast provides an endpoint of £1,368bn TME in 2025–26, not a like-for-like measure of real growth since 1997 by itself. Verify the interest series and 1997 endpoint before adding a numeric headline.

Interaction: one timeline, one decision or shock per stop, one accumulated picture. Any “what if this had not happened?” overlay must separate mechanical accounting from a genuine estimated counterfactual. Do not imply all nominal spending growth is discretionary policy or all interest costs result from one government.

Status: concept recorded for a distinct follow-up. Historical data extraction, attribution and a published timeline have not been completed. It is not added as another layer to the current five-step walkthrough.
